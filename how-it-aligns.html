The alignment gap

The plan and Tuesday’s task live in different worlds.

Most businesses have an annual plan and a task list. Nothing reliable sits between them. Strategy drifts, not because people are lazy, but because the goal never reaches the work, and the work never reports back. 1:90 Align is the line between the two.

Without the line

Leadership talks about the year. Teams work a list that may or may not connect. Each department translates the plan in its own way. Busy is not the same as aligned.

With 1:90 Align

One Arc. Three Waves. Weekly Milestones. Daily Tasks. The same chain, read from the board or from the person doing the work.

The cascade

One Arc. Then the work that proves it.

Scroll. The goal breaks downward. Then the truth comes back up. Orange sets the plan. Blue reports what is actually happening.

01 — Top down

One Arc. Not a shortlist.

Leadership sets a single strategic goal for 90 days. One Arc at a time. Focus beats breadth. The Arc Leader owns it and asks a blunt question: are we going to achieve this Arc?

Success criteria, the resources, and the three Waves are settled before the Arc is published. After that, the company is not choosing a new priority every Monday. It is delivering this one. The glossary is the shared definition. The framework is the full method.

02 — Three Waves

Plan, build, deliver.

The Arc breaks into three 30-day Waves. In the software they are named Foundation, Build and Complete. Same sequence the business already uses: plan, then build, then deliver.

  • Wave 1, Foundation. Plan the ground the quarter stands on.
  • Wave 2, Build. Do the work that makes the Arc real.
  • Wave 3, Complete. Deliver, then review before the next Arc.

A Team Leader owns the Wave. At the end of each one, the team holds a Wave Review and only then commits to the next.

03 — Weekly

Achieved, or not.

Each Wave holds a handful of Milestones. Typically two to four. They are binary: achieved, or not achieved. No percentage-complete to hide in.

Once a week the team holds a Compass Check. Each Milestone is looked at in the open. On track, at risk, or done. A risk named on Thursday is cheaper than a surprise in the Wave Review. The team owns the Milestone. The Arc Leader can see it without calling a meeting to ask.

04 — Daily

A Tuesday task with a line of sight.

Tasks are the daily unit of work. One owner. Linked upward. In the software a Task cannot be created unless it is linked to a Milestone. If it does not connect, it does not belong in the system.

Illustrative line, not a client: Draft the one-pager → Offer agreed → Wave 1 Foundation → Launch the new service line. The person doing the work can see that line. So can the Arc Leader, looking down.

05 — Bottom up

The same line, travelled back.

Progress, risk and capacity do not wait for the end of the quarter. Tasks roll up into the Milestone. Milestones roll into the Wave. Waves roll into the Arc. The dashboard the Arc Leader opens is that roll-up, not a separate story.

The Ripple each morning surfaces blockers in 15 minutes. The Compass Check each week checks the Milestones and the capacity for the week ahead. The Capacity Planner keeps the plan honest: who is available, who is already overloaded, and what leave and bank holidays do to the hours people can actually focus on the Arc.

Where the two directions meet

Alignment is not a poster. It is three things, shared.

Top down without a return path is a broadcast. Bottom up without an Arc is a busy team. They meet when the company shares a rhythm, a source of truth, and a language.

Who sees what

Four seats. One chain.

Nobody gets a different version of the truth. They get the altitude that matches the work they own.

Leadership · Arc Leader

Sets the Arc. Reads the roll-up.

“Are we going to achieve this Arc?”

  • Defines the one 90-day goal, the success criteria and the three Waves.
  • Publishes the Arc. Does not run a second priority beside it.
  • Sees Wave progress, Milestone risk and capacity without chasing updates.
  • Facilitates the planning at the start and the reset at the end.
Team Leader

Owns the Wave.

“Are we going to complete this Wave?”

  • Sets the weekly Milestones and assigns Tasks that link to them.
  • Runs the daily Ripple and the weekly Compass Check.
  • Uses the Capacity Planner before the week is full, not after it breaks.
  • Brings a clear Wave Review: done, not done, and what that means for the Arc.
Team member · Worker

Owns the Task. Sees why.

“Does the work in front of me move the Arc?”

  • Takes daily Tasks that already link to a Milestone, a Wave and the Arc.
  • Answers the three Ripple questions against that Arc, not a private list.
  • Raises a blocker the same day. Waiting is how drift starts.
  • Knows which week’s Milestone their Tuesday belongs to.

One rhythm

The ceremonies that keep the line honest.

The 90-day cadence is plan, execute, review, reset. Inside it, the week and the day have their own meetings. Same names, every team.

  1. Once per Arc

    Arc planning

    The Arc Leader sets the one goal and the three Waves. Resources are named before the Arc is published.

  2. Every morning · 15 min

    Ripple

    What moved the Arc yesterday? What moves it today? What is blocking it? Not a status tour.

  3. Every week

    Compass Check

    Milestones: achieved, or not yet. Risks named. Next week sized to real capacity.

  4. End of 30 days

    Wave Review

    The Team Leader closes the Wave against the Arc, then the next Wave is committed.

  5. End of 90 days

    Retrospective

    Review the Arc. Reset. One new Arc. The cadence starts again.

Before the call

A few straight answers.

What is an Arc?

An Arc is the one strategic goal for 90 days. A business runs one Arc at a time. Every Wave, Milestone and Task traces back to it. The Arc Leader owns the outcome.

What are the three Waves?

Each Arc contains three 30-day Waves. In the software they are Foundation, Build and Complete: plan, then build, then deliver. A Team Leader owns the Wave.

What is a Ripple?

A Ripple is the 15-minute daily standup. Three questions: What did I complete yesterday that moved us toward the Arc? What am I doing today that moves us toward the Arc? Is there anything blocking progress toward the Arc?

What is a Compass Check?

The Compass Check is the weekly meeting. The team reviews each Milestone — achieved, or not yet — names anything at risk, and checks capacity for the week ahead. The daily version of that honesty is the Ripple.

Who gets trained?

Everyone in the business. Arc Leaders, Team Leaders and Workers learn the same method, and they implement it while they train. Coaches who install 1:90 Align with clients take Coach Certification. The founding cohort is £1,499.

How is this different from EOS or OKRs?

OKRs describe what to achieve. 1:90 Align adds the line from that goal to today’s task. EOS uses a quarterly rhythm and a set of Rocks. 1:90 Align forces one Arc, is built in the UK, and is not a US franchise at £36,000 or more a year. The longer comparison is on the comparison page.
