Act 1 · Before

Nobody was on the same job.

Harbour & Co. does client work. Proposals, delivery, the usual. The people are capable. The company is not pointed at one thing. The cards show the mess: crossed wires, clashing colours, and work that will not sit in one place.

  • Goals set, and not heard
  • Teams working in silos
  • Priorities that clash
  • Updates lost in email and Slack
  • Deadlines that slip
  • People overloaded, or idle
  • A board that never sees real progress
  • Meetings that run long and decide little
Chief executive

Helen

The goal leaves the room. The work does not follow.

She sets the year’s aims in January. By March, sales is selling a new offer and delivery is still finishing the old one. She hears about the clash in a board pack, weeks after the people doing the work have felt it.

Team leader

Raj

Monday is a tug of war, and Friday’s update is already buried.

Sales has promised a date. His people are already full. The note he wrote on Friday is somewhere in Slack. He cannot see who has room, so he guesses, and the guess is usually wrong.

Worker

Sam

Three urgents, or nothing at all.

Some days every message says it matters. None of them say which one serves the goal. Other days she waits, because nobody has said what done looks like. The long meeting does not settle it.

The turn

They adopt 1:90 Align.

Same people. Same clients. The work stops living in seven places. Scroll, and the picture follows: the clash drops out, the wires uncross, and the scattered cards become one line.

If your device is set to reduce motion, the picture stays still and shows both states together.

The clash becomes an order.

Three priorities that all claimed to be first become three Waves. Foundation, then Build, then Complete. A deadline is 30 days, or it is the Milestone inside the week.

The line runs both ways.

The Arc breaks down to today’s Task. Progress, risk and load travel back up the same line. Helen can see it without rebuilding a pack from inboxes.

Act 2 · After

The same work, on one process.

Each pain in the first act has a place in 1:90 Align. The names below are the method. The people are still the illustration.

Before · goals nobody hears

The Arc gives one direction.

Helen had aims. The building did not. The Arc is one North Star for 90 days. One direction. Sales, delivery and operations can all point at it, or they can see that they are not.

Before · deadlines slip, priorities clash

Waves and Milestones give the time a shape.

A Wave is 30 days. A Milestone is about seven days, a weekly checkpoint. Foundation, Build and Complete run in order, so three rival “firsts” become a sequence. A date is a Wave or a Milestone, not a hope in a slide.

Before · nobody knows today

Tasks make today obvious.

A Task is the daily piece of work, owned by a person, and it still points up at a Milestone. Sam can say what she is doing today, and why it belongs. Work that does not connect does not get to pretend it does.

Before · overloaded, or idle

Capacity shows the load before the promise.

1:90 Align counts capacity in points, with a focus percentage for how much of a person’s time sits on this Arc. A standard Wave is built around 55 points across the working days. Focus at 100%, 75%, 50% or 25% scales that share. In this illustration only, Sam’s focus on the Arc is half her week. That half is part of the story, not a measured result. The 55-point Wave is how the product counts. Raj can see who is full, and who has room, before he says yes.

Before · lost in email and Slack

The alignment board is the one source of truth.

Team roles, risks, success metrics, agreements and decisions live on the alignment board. A risk has a likelihood, an impact and a mitigation. An agreement about how decisions get made is written down. The long thread stops being the memory of the company.

Before · silos, and a blind board

Down from the Arc. Back up from the Task.

The way down is Arc, then Wave, then Milestone, then Task. The way back up is the same line: a Task finishes, the Milestone shows it, the Wave shows the Milestone, the Arc shows the Wave. The teams still exist. They no longer keep a private priority. The board sees the picture the team updated, not a reconstruction from three weeks ago.

Before · meetings that wander

The ceremonies keep one rhythm.

Each meeting has a job and a length. The Daily Ripple is 15 minutes. The Compass Check is weekly, 30 minutes. A Mid-Wave check-in sits around days 15, 45 and 75. The Wave Review closes each Wave. The Arc Review asks, on day 90, whether the North Star happened. The detail is just below.

Before · a different word on every floor

Training gives the company one language.

Milestone means the same thing in Helen’s meeting and on Sam’s list. Arc Leaders, Team Leaders and Workers learn the method, and they implement it while they train. The programme is on the training page.

Helen, now

One sentence for the quarter.

She can say the North Star, and so can the board. She is the Arc sponsor: accountable for the Arc, clearing blockers rather than hunting through inboxes for the truth.

Raj, now

The month and the week have edges.

He runs the team’s Wave, the Milestones and the ceremonies. Capacity, in points and focus percentage, is on the table before a date is promised. Monday has somewhere to go.

Sam, now

Today is named.

Her Task is on the board, linked up to the Arc. The Ripple is fifteen minutes. She is not guessing which urgent is real, and she is not waiting in silence for someone to invent a job.

The rhythm

Meetings with a job, and an ending.

These are the ceremonies in 1:90 Align. The lengths are the format, not a claim about what a company will save.

  1. Daily · 15 minutes

    Daily Ripple

    What moved the Arc, what is next, what is blocking. The daily version of honesty. It replaces the hour that used to wander.

  2. Weekly · 30 minutes

    Compass Check

    The Arc sponsor and the leads. Are we on track, and does the course need a correction. Milestones are achieved, or they are not.

  3. Days 15, 45 and 75

    Mid-Wave check-in

    A look in the middle of the Wave, so a month does not pass before anyone says the work has drifted.

  4. Every 30 days · about 2 hours

    Wave Review

    The full team, at the end of the Wave. What worked, and what did not, before the next 30 days are named.

  5. Day 90 · half a day

    Arc Review

    Team and stakeholders. Did we hit the North Star, and what should the next Arc learn. Then the cycle starts again.

Before the call

A few straight answers.

Is Harbour & Co. a real client?

No. It is a fictional 40-person UK services business, standing in for problems we see often. Nothing on this page is a testimonial or a measured result.

What is an Arc?

An Arc is the one strategic goal for 90 days. A business runs one Arc at a time. Every Wave, Milestone and Task traces back to it. Helen, in the story, is accountable for it as the Arc sponsor.

What are the three Waves?

Each Arc contains three 30-day Waves. On this site they are Foundation, Build and Complete: set the ground, do the work, finish it. Milestones sit inside a Wave as weekly checkpoints of about seven days.
